01 · The problem in one picture

Every new load must be designed, studied and approved before it connects

A factory, data centre, EV depot or large building cannot draw new power until engineers show the connection is safe and the grid can carry it.

Likely questions

Isn't the real bottleneck transformers and construction?

Part of the wait is hardware and construction. But in Hydro One's recent large-load connections, studies and cost estimates took about 41% of the time from the first impact studies to energization (Table 1, p. 23). OEB report↗ That is the part NewCo targets; equipment and construction stay with contractors.

Who decides whether a site can connect?

The utility and, in Ontario, the IESO run the impact studies; ESA reviews plans and inspects. Those roles belong to them by law. An engineering firm prepares and seals what they review, and answers their comments. Idea memo, step table

02 · A short glossary

Three voltage levels, six parties

Voltage decides who may design a connection; the parties decide who pays, who builds and who approves.

The voltage ladder
▲
HVHigh voltage · transmission
Bulk power across the province. The largest sites connect here, studied by the utility and the grid operator.
MVMedium voltage · distribution
Feeds factories, large buildings, campuses, depots. Drawings must be stamped by a P.Eng. Toronto Hydro↗
LVLow voltage · homes, small shops
Below 750 V a licensed electrical contractor can design and install the service in Ontario. ESA bulletin↗

Owner or developer

Owner of an existing site, or developer of a new project. Pays for the connection, signs the engineering contract and carries the cost of waiting.

EPC / electrical contractor

Builds the project. Often hires the engineer of record and absorbs late rework. A separate party from the developer.

Engineering firm NewCo

Designs the connection, seals the package as engineer of record, answers the utility's comments.

Local utility

Owns the local wires (Toronto Hydro, Hydro One, Hydro-Québec). Reviews and approves every package.

IESO · grid operator

Runs Ontario's provincial grid. With the utility, runs the system impact studies for large connections.

ESA · safety authority

Ontario's Electrical Safety Authority. Reviews electrical plans and inspects before the site is energized.

NewCo would be the engineering firm in this picture: hired by the owner or developer, reviewed by the utility, the IESO and ESA.

Likely questions

What is the difference between the utility and the IESO?

The local utility owns the wires the site plugs into and approves the connection. The IESO operates Ontario's whole grid; for large loads it and the utility run the impact studies. Neither buys the applicant's engineering; both review it. Idea memo, step table

Why keep the developer and the EPC apart?

They buy for different reasons. The developer or owner pays for and waits on the connection; the EPC runs construction, hires the engineer of record and absorbs rework. On utility-scale projects about 80% of connection packages go to an outside engineer of record. Expert call: Haris Rashid, TruGrid Which of them pays most readily is a sprint question.

03 · What gets submitted

A connection package, sealed by an engineer of record

Connection package

Everything the utility needs to decide whether, and how, a site connects: application forms, single-line diagram, load and equipment data, site plan and the engineer's stamp. Later come detailed drawings and test reports.

Engineer of record and the seal

The licensed engineer who seals the package and stays responsible for it. A seal means the work was prepared by, or under the supervision of, the licence holder. PEO↗

AI does not move the responsibility

PEO holds engineers fully responsible for AI output, and Québec's order says plans not prepared under the engineer's direction and immediate supervision cannot be sealed. PEO AI advisory↗ OIQ↗

…as long as ultimately there is some sign-off responsibility that is given to a licensed person or engineerHaris Rashid, Pre-construction lead @ TruGrid
On the grid the signature is the product: whoever seals the package carries the legal responsibility for it.

Likely questions

Could software alone produce an acceptable package?

Not legally: only a licence holder who prepared or supervised the work may seal it. PEO↗ Before signing AI work an engineer needs full traceability of inputs, results validated against standard tools (PSCAD, ETAP, PSS/E), and the ability to rerun critical cases. Expert call: Haris Rashid, TruGrid

What does the utility do with the package?

A completeness review, then impact studies, an offer to connect with a cost estimate, and later design approval. Missing items found late send the file back. OEB report↗

Likely questions

Which part of this can a private firm shorten?

Not the utility's studies and not construction. A firm controls the application, its completeness, the comment rounds and the untimed design step. The OEB names engineering design as a common source of delay. OEB report↗

Isn't the regulator already fixing this?

It is rewriting the rules: after Ontario's June 2025 directive the OEB proposes one standard connection procedure. Ontario directive↗ OEB↗ Standard procedures raise the cost of an incomplete submission, which makes first-time completeness more valuable, not less. Idea memo, Timing

How long do impact studies take elsewhere?

A former BC Hydro engineer told us system impact review can take “6 to 8 months even.” Expert call: Pritam Haldar, GridWise

07 · Root cause two: rework

Unclear requirements send first submissions back for rework

No single, clear checklist

Each utility has its own drawing requirements and templates, and the checklists applicants get are generic. Gaps surface only when a utility engineer finally opens the file. Expert call: Sheldon Pinto

Relatively limited changes in information often result in restarting the assessment timeline from the beginning.Ontario Energy Board, connection framework report-back, 2026 OEB↗
I submitted 6 months ago, now it's too late… Now you'll have to wait another 4 months before my engineer designs a drawing for it.Sheldon Pinto, Customer Connections Intake Manager @ Toronto Hydro
Incomplete first submissions are the most avoidable delay: one missing drawing can add four months to a file that already waited six.

Likely questions

Why don't utilities publish one complete checklist?

Ontario has more than 50 distributors, with “significant variations” between them. OEB↗ The OEB now proposes one standard connection procedure; until it lands, applicants work from generic checklists. Idea memo

How often do first submissions fail?

Our sources do not measure it. Approvals are rarely refused; they are slow. Expert calls Ontario first-time-complete rates are a sprint question, and a high rate would weaken the thesis.

Can't an experienced consultant avoid this?

Largely, which is why owners pay for them: a first-time consultant takes two to three months for what an experienced one does in one. Expert call: Pritam Haldar, GridWise The scarce asset is knowledge of each utility's rules.

09 · The boundary

Where an engineering firm can actually move the clock

An engineering firm cannot shorten the utility's review, but it controls everything before it: whether the package is complete the first time, how fast comments are answered, and whether scarce engineers design or chase status.

Likely questions

So how many months could NewCo save?

Not yet measured. Practitioners see 25–30% fewer engineering hours and 70–80% of documents drafted by AI Expert call: Haris Rashid, TruGrid; the utility side advises claiming only 10–15%, at most 25%. Expert call: Sheldon Pinto Months saved per project is a sprint question.

Doesn't a faster applicant just hit the same queue?

Partly, yes: the review is not NewCo's to shorten. But an incomplete file can restart the assessment from the beginning OEB↗, and a missing drawing found after six months costs another four. A complete package avoids re-entering the queue.

10 · The idea

NewCo: agents draft, check and track; a licensed engineer seals

The steps in a typical connection packagePhase 1, where NewCo winsPhase 2, where it grows as engineer of record
NewCo sells signed engineering, not software: agents let one engineer carry far more packages, and the seal is what owners pay for.

Likely questions

Isn't the seal a liability risk for an AI firm?

The liability is the same as for any firm: PEO holds the sealing engineer fully responsible for AI output. PEO↗ The memo's safeguards: full traceability of every input, results checked against PSS/E, PSCAD or ETAP, critical cases rerun, and an independent audit of the agent workflow before NewCo takes on formal studies. Idea memo Insurance cost and terms are a sprint question.

What does NewCo not do?

It does not run the utility's studies or approvals (those belong to the utility, the IESO and ESA), it does not build or buy equipment, and it does not negotiate the owner's commercial agreement. Idea memo

How is this different from Rodan or ORAO?

Same deliverable and seal, different production system: they sell most of these packages in Ontario by conventional means and are capacity-bound. Rodan↗ ORAO↗

If utilities standardize, does the problem go away?

Standardization raises the cost of an incomplete submission rather than removing the work, and the OEB notes engineering design still has no required timeline. OEB↗

Likely questions

Why won’t Rodan simply adopt AI?

It might: an Ontario firm adopting AI, or being acquired by one that has it, is a named risk. Incentives cut against it. Firms bill by the hour, the average bills 62% of staff time and nets about 20% BQE↗, and on public work open books and fixed rate cards make AI costs hard to recover.

What if Monterra comes to Ontario?

It would need a PEO Certificate of Authorization PEO↗ and a move up from commercial LV/MV work. Monterra announcing MV/HV or Canadian work is a signal that would change our view; it is on the sprint’s watch list.
