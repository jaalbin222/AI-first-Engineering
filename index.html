The problem

Medium- and high-voltage connections are slow, and much of the wait is engineering and review.

Every new MV or HV load, whether a factory, data centre, EV depot or large building, has to be designed, studied and approved before anything is built. Demand is rising faster than anyone can review it.

Close to half

of the time from the first impact studies to energization in Hydro One's recent large-load connections went to those studies (the System Impact Assessment and Customer Impact Assessment) and to cost estimates. Add the feasibility and application work that comes first, and roughly half of the total timeline passes before anything is built.

Not enough engineers on either side

Utility connection teams are sized by multi-year rate plans and can't grow when requests jump. At Toronto Hydro, a senior engineer carries 20 to 30 high-voltage projects at once, and a medium-voltage engineer up to 60.

Per Toronto Hydro's Customer Connections Intake Manager (practitioner interview).

Requirements differ by utility, so packages come back

Each utility has its own drawing requirements and templates, checklists are generic, and comments arrive in rounds. Missing items often surface only after a utility engineer opens the file.

Practitioner interviews; OEB.

Coordination runs on email, phone and paper

A utility connection engineer spends an estimated 30–40% of the day on communication. There is no shared tracker applicants can check, so they email and call for updates.

Per Toronto Hydro's Customer Connections Intake Manager (practitioner interview; estimate).

"Relatively limited changes in information often result in restarting the assessment timeline from the beginning."Ontario Energy Board, Streamlining Ontario's Electricity Connection Framework. How it works

Agents draft, check and track. Engineers review, approve and seal.

Pylon's production system is a set of role agents, each mirroring an accountable position in a conventional consulting practice, supervised by licensed engineers who hold the only authority to approve and seal.

Role agents originate the work from a single project record, so a change propagates everywhere.

Learning from every comment

Every utility comment becomes a check on the next package.

Requirements differ by utility and are rarely written down. Pylon records what each utility's reviewers actually ask for and checks every package against it before submission, so packages arrive complete the first time.

  1. Capture the utility's comment letter or markup.
  2. Classify it by discipline, type and root cause.
  3. Map it to an existing rule, or propose a new one.
  4. Validate: a senior engineer accepts, edits or rejects the rule.
  5. Apply the rule to every live package for that utility.
  6. Measure first-time-complete rate and comment rounds per utility.

    What Pylon doesn't do

    • Build, buy equipment or do the utility's grid-side work. Crews, equipment and grid-side work stay with contractors, suppliers and the utility.
    • Run the utility's or IESO's impact studies, or approve anything. Those belong to the utility, the IESO and ESA. We make them faster by sending complete, accurate inputs the first time.
    • Negotiate or sign the commercial agreement. That is the owner's decision; we give you the technical basis.

    Why an engineering firm, not software

    On the grid, the signature is the product.

    Owners and EPCs connecting MV and HV loads aren't shopping for a tool. They're buying a named engineer who takes responsibility for the design.

    Signed results, not software output

    You receive engineer-sealed packages in the utility's own forms and formats. The firm that signs carries the professional responsibility, which is often why buyers outsource this work in the first place.

    A fixed price per package

    Incumbent fees are fixed but scoped from hours, with extra comment rounds billed hourly. Pylon charges a fixed price per signed package with comment rounds included, so the time agents save becomes speed for you rather than billable hours.

    One accountable counterpart

    The problem is the handoffs between applicants, consultants and utilities. A firm doing the work removes handoffs that a tracking tool can only log, and the utility deals with one engineer of record.

    Capacity you can't hire fast enough

    A tool speeds up an existing engineer, but you still have to find and hire one. Pylon is the added capacity, and available capacity is often what decides who gets the work.

    Trust and traceability

    No black boxes. Every number traces back to its inputs.

    Before signing AI-assisted work, an engineer needs a full trace of every input and assumption, results from standard tools, and the ability to rerun critical cases. That is how Pylon is built.

    Every number tied to its inputs

    An append-only ledger records every agent action and human decision. Each number in a sealed package carries its chain of inputs, assumptions, tool versions and model versions.

    Models you can rerun in ETAP, PSS/E or PSCAD

    Studies run in the tools utilities already accept. Every study ships with a rerun bundle: the native model file, case settings and the script that reproduces each result.

    An independent check before anything is filed

    The checker is separate from the agent that drafted the work, and deterministic rule checks run without any model at all. A model checking its own work is not a check.

    The engineer holds the seal

    The plan: every package is sealed by professional engineers licensed in Ontario (PEO) and Québec (OIQ). PEO's digital signature must remain under the sole control of its holder (PEO), so signing happens outside the agent system and no agent holds signing credentials.

    Your data stays controlled

    Enterprise model contracts with zero data retention and no training on your inputs, hosting and storage in a Canadian region, and isolation between clients, in line with the OIQ's guidance on responsible AI use.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Is Pylon a software company?

    No. Pylon is an engineering firm. We sell finished, engineer-sealed engineering packages at a fixed price per package. AI agents are how we produce the work; the deliverable and the responsibility are an engineer's.

    Who signs my package, and who is responsible for it?

    A licensed professional engineer who is Pylon's engineer of record for your project. They approve the design basis at kickoff, review the work and the checker's findings, approve the package at issue and apply the seal personally. Agents never hold signing credentials.

    Is AI-assisted engineering allowed in Ontario and Québec?

    Yes, when a licensed engineer directs the work and takes responsibility for it. PEO holds engineers fully responsible for AI output (PEO AI advisory), and the OIQ has published a guide on responsible AI use (OIQ guide). In Québec, plans must be prepared under the engineer's direction and immediate supervision, so our engineer sets the design basis at the start and supervises through review as the work proceeds.

    What does the utility receive?

    A standard engineer-sealed submission in the utility's own forms and formats, through its normal intake channel. The engineer of record answers for it like any other sealed package.

    Which engineering software do you use?

    The tools utilities already accept: ETAP or SKM PowerTools for MV studies, arc flash and coordination; Siemens PSS/E for anything the IESO or a transmitter will review; PSCAD for transient studies when required; AutoCAD for drawings. We don't write our own solvers, and every study comes with a model file your reviewer can open and rerun.

    Do you run the utility's impact studies?

    No. The utility and, for larger loads, the IESO run their impact assessments. We supply the data and models (for example PSS/E files where required) and answer their questions, so the inputs are complete the first time.

    Do you build or supply equipment?

    No. Pylon does engineering, never physical construction. We write the specifications and review submittals; you or your EPC buy the equipment, and contractors and the utility do the physical work.

    How do you handle our drawings and data?

    Enterprise model contracts with zero data retention and no training on inputs, hosting and storage in a Canadian region, and isolation between clients. Utility system data supplied under NDA is handled under the utility's terms.

    How is the price set?

    A fixed price per package, quoted from the package's scope: the utility, request type, service and site records. Comment rounds are included, and billing follows milestones.

    Do you work on generation and storage connections?

    We start with load connections. Generation and storage connections go through the same engineering steps and are our next service area.
